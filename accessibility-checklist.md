# Accessibility research checklist, by study phase

Use this at the start of each phase. Tick what is done, and note in the protocol anything you have decided not to do and why.

Numbers in italics refer to the principles in `contentv3.md`:
1 Safety · 2 Trustworthiness and transparency · 3 Peer support · 4 Collaboration and mutuality · 5 Empowerment, voice and choice · 6 Cultural, historical and gender issues · 7 "None of these fit" (filing label, for gaps in the principle set)

---

## Planning

- [ ] Disabled people and community partners were involved before the research question was fixed. Record who set the question and what changed. *(4)*
- [ ] Decision rights are written down: who can change the protocol, who approves publication, and how disagreements are settled. *(4)*
- [ ] Paid time is budgeted for lived-experience co-researchers, including preparation, travel and debrief. *(3)*
- [ ] Instruments were checked against the community's language, and against whether they were validated with disabled people. *(6)*
- [ ] The methods section says who the study does and does not speak for. *(6)*
- [ ] A lone-working and home-visit protocol covers access, check-in times and an exit plan. *(1)*

## Training

- [ ] Everyone on the team has been trained on the access needs they are likely to meet, including communication, sensory and mobility needs. *(1)*
- [ ] Interviewers know how to offer a pause, a break, a change of format and a proxy, and how to do so without asking for a reason. *(5)*
- [ ] Peer researchers have agreed in writing what they are asked to do and what they are not expected to speak for. *(3)*
- [ ] Team members know how to respond if a participant discloses distress, and who to contact. *(1, 3)*

## Recruitment

- [ ] Recruitment materials are in the formats participants said they need (for example, easy-read, audio, large print, or a sign-language version). *(2)*
- [ ] Each person is asked which access needs they have (format, location, timing, sensory conditions, support person) before scheduling. *(1)*
- [ ] Recruitment is separated from any service decision the participant depends on, and the participant is told this. *(5)*
- [ ] Participants can take part in a way that does not require them to disclose their diagnosis or status, unless it is needed for the study. *(1, 6)*

## Consent

- [ ] The information sheet is given in at least two formats, and understanding is checked with a short teach-back. *(2)*
- [ ] The first paragraph says what cannot be promised: confidentiality limits, who can see raw data, and how long it is kept. *(2)*
- [ ] Alternatives are offered, not a single method with an opt-out: interview, written response, group, or proxy. *(5)*
- [ ] Consent is treated as ongoing, not a one-off signature. The participant is told they can pause or stop at any point. *(5)*
- [ ] Withdrawal is explained: how to withdraw, until when, and what happens to data already collected. *(5)*

## Data collection

- [ ] The venue, format and timing are the participant's choice, with a fallback that does not penalise them. *(1, 5)*
- [ ] The session can be paused, redirected or ended by the participant. Each time this happens, log it. *(4)*
- [ ] Emotional labour is accounted for: there is a debrief for the researcher, and the participant is offered support. *(1, 3)*
- [ ] Sensory and communication conditions are checked at the start (lighting, noise, recording quality, device, captioning). *(1)*
- [ ] A safety check-in is scheduled after any session that involved painful material. *(1)*

## Data management

- [ ] Recordings, transcripts and notes are stored with access controls that match the sensitivity of the content. *(2)*
- [ ] Participants can review and correct their transcript or notes, and a deadline is set that they can use. *(5)*
- [ ] Any identifying detail that could expose a participant (location, workplace, small community) has been removed or agreed with them. *(2)*
- [ ] The data retention period and the destruction date are recorded in the protocol. *(2)*

## Analysis

- [ ] Coding categories are checked for deficit framing (for example, "non-adherence", "burden", "low engagement"). Rewrite any that do not reflect participants' own terms. *(6)*
- [ ] Draft interpretations are sent to participants or partners, and any disagreement is recorded in the write-up. *(2, 4)*
- [ ] A review meeting with partners happens before the findings are finalised. *(4)*
- [ ] Findings that could be read as a claim about a whole group are checked against the data and qualified. *(6)*

## Dissemination

- [ ] The write-up does not label disabled people as a problem to be solved. Terms are the ones participants prefer. *(6)*
- [ ] A plain-language summary is published with the paper. *(2)*
- [ ] Participants and peer researchers are credited as they want to be, including whether they want to be identified. *(3, 5)*
- [ ] Events and online platforms meet the access needs participants named. *(1)*
- [ ] Participants have been told how the findings were used, and what changed as a result. *(4)*

## Aftercare

- [ ] Participants who asked for follow-up have received it, and anyone who needed support knows how to access it. *(1)*
- [ ] Any mitigation the team agreed has been checked against what happened in practice. *(7)*
- [ ] Anything that did not fit the six principles has been recorded, with the reason. *(7)*

---

## Review at the end of the study

- [ ] Which items were skipped, and why?
- [ ] Which items were hardest to do in practice, and what would make them easier next time?
- [ ] What did participants and partners say was missing?
- [ ] What changes should be made to the principle set?
