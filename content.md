# Common Ground: content

All text the game uses: principles, dilemmas, complications and testimony cards. Copy it exactly as written, unless the facilitator asks for a change. `plan.md` describes how the game uses this content.

Generated from `cards.html`. Counts: 7 principles, 30 dilemmas, 20 complications per dilemma (600 in total), 4 testimony cards.

## 1. Principles

The first six are the categories. Every dilemma belongs to one of them. The seventh is a filing label only.

1. **Safety**
   - Guiding question: Does this person feel safe in this room, in this format, with you — and do you?
   - Principle: Everyone involved feels physically and psychologically safe.
2. **Trustworthiness and transparency**
   - Guiding question: Does the participant know what happens to what they say, who hears it, and what you cannot promise?
   - Principle: Decisions are made openly, so that trust can be built and kept.
3. **Peer support**
   - Guiding question: Is anyone here because they have been through it — and are they paid, supported and credited?
   - Principle: People with shared experience are a route to safety and hope.
4. **Collaboration and mutuality**
   - Guiding question: Who set the question, who controls the pace, and who decides when it stops?
   - Principle: Power differences are levelled, and the work is done in partnership.
5. **Empowerment, voice and choice**
   - Guiding question: Is the choice on offer a real one, or a choice between taking part as designed and not at all?
   - Principle: People's strengths are recognised, and choice is real rather than nominal.
6. **Cultural, historical and gender issues**
   - Guiding question: Whose norms shaped this method, and what history does this community have with people like you?
   - Principle: Stereotypes are moved past; historical trauma is recognised and addressed.
7. **None of these fit**
   - Guiding question: So what does it serve? A principle set that cannot name your fix is a finding about the set.
   - Principle: The six do not name what this fix actually does.

## 2. Dilemmas

Numbered 1 to 30. Dilemma numbers match the order in `cards.html`. Each dilemma lists its category, lifecycle phase, description, three prompts for a stalled round, and its 20 complications.

### Category 1: Safety

#### 1. The lone visit

- **Category:** Safety
- **Phase:** Data collection
- **Description:** A follow-up interview is at the participant's home, in an area your team has been warned about. You are going alone, and the only person who knows the address is the participant.
- **Prompts for a stalled round:**
  - Who needs to know where you are, and when should they expect to hear from you?
  - What would make this visit safe enough to go ahead with, and what would make it not?
  - What would you do if the participant asked you to leave?
- **Complications:**
  1. The address is not on the calendar, and nobody else has it.
  2. Your phone has no signal in the street where the participant lives.
  3. The participant's partner is expected to be at home.
  4. You arrive ten minutes early and the street is empty.
  5. The check-in call is scheduled for after the visit.
  6. The participant has been drinking and wants to talk about the worst night.
  7. The recorder battery is almost flat and there is no spare.
  8. The team's safety protocol has not been updated since the last incident.
  9. Your co-researcher has been pulled onto another project this week.
  10. The participant offers you a lift home from a stranger's car.
  11. The participant's neighbour keeps looking through the window.
  12. You have no way to leave the house quickly from this room.
  13. The participant is expecting a longer visit than your schedule allows.
  14. The visit overruns, and the next appointment is across town.
  15. Nobody has told the participant that you are a researcher and not a support worker.
  16. The participant has a dog that reacts when the door opens.
  17. There is no lone-working policy that covers home visits.
  18. The participant asks you to stay for dinner.
  19. The participant mentions the person who harmed them is coming round later.
  20. You have been asked to take a second, unplanned visit the same afternoon.

## 3. Testimony cards

Read aloud in full on testimony rounds. Nobody plays a complication on these.

### 1. I didn't know it would go this deep

> I signed up for a study about access to services. Then they started asking about the most painful things in my life, things I don't normally talk about. The researcher was kind, but I wasn't prepared. I kept going because I didn't want to let them down. Afterwards I couldn't sleep.

Prompts for a stalled round:
- Was the consent process honest about emotional risk, or only about data?
- What should have been in place for the hours after the interview?
- What made it hard to stop, and who built that in?

### 2. I said yes, but I didn't feel I could say no

> They asked me to a co-design session about disability services. I was glad to be asked. When I arrived there were government officials and service managers in the room. I smiled and nodded a lot. Inside I was shrinking. I wanted to contribute, but not like that.

Prompts for a stalled round:
- Who was in that room, and who decided they would be?
- Was consent ongoing, or was it a signature collected once?
- What would have made leaving a real option?

### 3. They asked me again, and again

> I've told my story in so many projects. They all want to hear how bad it was, and then they disappear. Nothing changes. Now they've come back for a follow-up and want all the same things. I thought I was done.

Prompts for a stalled round:
- What has this person had back from any of it?
- Is this participation, or extraction with better manners?
- What would an honest re-approach have said first?

### 4. No one asked what I needed

> I'm Deaf. They sent me a consent form to sign without asking whether I needed anything. The interview was on a video call, the captions didn't work, and there was no interpreter. I tried my best. At the end they thanked me. I'm not sure they understood me.

Prompts for a stalled round:
- At what point should access have been asked about, and by whom?
- What did taking part cost this person that the study will never record?
- What was assumed about how they communicate?
